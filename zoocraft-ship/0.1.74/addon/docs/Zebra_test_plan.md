# Zebra test plan (0.1.74)

The third animal of the Animal wave is the Zebra (spec 5.2: "zebra | Zebra | Coordinated herd movement | Savanna mixing and broad grazing"). Zebras live in herds: by day they graze spread out, now and then walk off together in single file, and rest in pairs; at night most lie down while one stands watch. Run at them and the herd barks the alarm, gallops off together, then stops and stares back. They graze the grass in their exhibit (enough grass gives them half their food), live in harems (one stallion with his mares; a second stallion fights him), and feed in rank order, so they need enough feeding places: the new Hay Rack has room for 4. Grain Rations (wheat or a hay bale) are their food. This build adds a new animal, a block, an item and textures, so fully close and reopen Minecraft after the build.

Needs: a superflat or test world in Creative with cheats on, a Zoo Workshop, a Habitat Marker, an Exhibit Clipboard, a few Livestock Crates, a Basic Feeder, a Puzzle Feeder, a Hanging Food Basket, a Water Trough with water, a fence, grass blocks, stone, wheat, hay bales, sticks and planks. A park day is 20 minutes of play; `/zoocraft:skip 1200` runs one park day now. A savanna is the place to find wild herds.

| 4 | Stand back (16 blocks or more) and watch a wild herd for a few minutes by day. | The herd does things together for a while, then changes. Grazing: spread out, heads right down with the muzzle in the grass a little in front of the forefeet, one foreleg a step ahead, the head jerking up a little as it tears each bite and the jaw chewing; now and then one takes a slow step or two on. Standing about: the tail swishes at flies, the ears flick, it looks round, sniffs the ground, shakes its head, stamps a forefoot twice, or rests a hind leg on the tip of its hoof with that hip dropped. Hoof, eating and snorting sounds now and then, not from every zebra at once. |
| 5 | Keep watching (a few minutes more). | Now and then the herd walks off in single file, 3 blocks apart, one behind another, the stallion at the rear; any foal walks right behind its mother. The others walk up to where the leader set off, each setting off once the one in front of it is on its way, and follow in its steps, trotting to catch up when they fall behind, then walking again; any zebra standing or grazing where the file is going steps off its way. At the end they spread out and graze again. Resting (more often around midday): some doze on their feet (head low, ears out to the sides, eyes shut, a hind hoof resting); pairs groom standing side by side facing opposite ways, nibbling each other's withers; pairs stand head to tail with one resting its chin on the other's back, eyes shut; one lies down on its chest (front end first: down onto its knees, then the back end) and may roll in the dust onto its back with its legs folded in the air, wriggling, then gets up (front end first: a foreleg out, the other, up like a sitting dog, then the push behind) and shakes from head to tail. Hooves, knees and hocks stay on the ground while it lies down, rolls and gets up. |
| 6 | Walk toward a wild herd (not sneaking). Then sneak up to it. Then hold a Grain Ration near it. | Not sneaking, they move off once you're within about 8 blocks. Sneaking, you can walk right up. Holding a Grain Ration (wheat or a hay bale work too), they come to you. Catch one with an empty Livestock Crate. |
| 7 | Sprint at a wild herd from about 15 blocks away. | One zebra barks the alarm (a high, hoarse call with the head and neck pushing up each time), the others stop and stare (heads up, ears pricked forward). Then the whole herd gallops off together the same way, with galloping hooves (one right behind another sets off a moment later, so none runs into another; one with a tree or a rock in its way swerves round it and runs on with the rest), slows to a trot and a walk, stops clear of the others, turns round and stares back at you; the stallion ends up nearest you. The legs keep time with the ground at each speed (no sliding). Run at them again straight away: after a fright they stay calm for about half a minute. Hit one, even right after a fright: the herd runs as well. |
| 8 | Watch a wild herd at night (`/time set night`) from about 10 blocks off, not sneaking. | Most lie down on their chests, legs folded under, now and then with the chin resting on the ground; some lie flat out on their side asleep, eyes shut, a foreleg twitching now and then. One stands watch, head high, looking slowly about, and the watch changes after a while. None lies down within about 8 blocks of you, and none keeps lying down, getting up and walking off again. |
