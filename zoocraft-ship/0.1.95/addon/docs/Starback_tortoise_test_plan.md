# Starback Tortoise test plan (0.1.95)

The second of the End pair, and the last custom animal, is the Starback Tortoise (spec 5.4: "Dark shell with star-like markings | Slow browsing and resting | End vegetation, warm pad, large retreat"). It is a fantasy tortoise built on the Indian star tortoise, drawn big so it's easy to see: a female's shell is about 0.8 blocks long. The End has no day or night, so it keeps to bouts: it rests under a low roof, comes out to bask on a Warm Pad till it's warm, then browses the chorus, and goes back to bask when it has cooled. Come close and it pulls into its shell with a hiss; it never runs. This version adds new entities, a new block, textures and a recipe, so close Minecraft fully and start it again before testing.

You need:
- a superflat world and a Wild Starback Tortoise Spawn Egg;
- a stack of end stone, end stone bricks, 3 magma blocks, 6 or more chorus plants and chorus flowers (from the Creative inventory: a chorus plant stands only on end stone or another chorus plant, a chorus flower only on end stone or a chorus plant), a few Mineral Substrate and some chorus fruit;
- a Zoo Workshop, a Stabilized Carrier (Dimensional Preparation research), a Habitat Marker, an Exhibit Clipboard and a Field Journal;
- a Basic Feeder, a Water Trough and a water bucket.

| # | Do this | You should see |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In Creative, use the spawn egg 3 or 4 times on the flat field. Look at them from all sides, close up. | A blocky tortoise with a black, high-domed shell in steps, a yellow star (a plus-shaped sparkle with a pale, faintly glowing centre) on each scute, pale olive-yellow legs, neck, head and tail. Males are clearly smaller (about two thirds of a female) and have a longer, thicker tail. A fall doesn't hurt them. |
| 2 | Stay in Creative and watch one for 5 minutes. | It plods very slowly, about a quarter of a block a second, legs stepping by turns, and turns slowly. Out in the open it basks: legs splayed out flat and neck stretched out along the ground, for up to 2 minutes (it warms slowly in the open). Then it looks about for chorus (none here) and grazes the ground, head down, plodding on 2 to 4 blocks between bites. After 3 to 5 minutes out it rests where it is, drawn halfway into its shell, for 1 and a half to 2 and a half minutes, then comes out again. Two never stand in the same block. |
| 3 | Build a low shelter near them: 4 end stone pillars 1 block high with a roof of end stone bricks 2 blocks over the ground (a lean-to or a little hut, open on one side). Watch the next rests. | At the end of a bout each walks under the roof to rest, and comes back to the same spot next time while it's free. With no roof near, it rests against a wall or a chorus stalk, else where it is. |
| 4 | Put a chorus plant on end stone and a chorus flower on end stone near them. Watch a few minutes. | Once one has basked it finds the chorus within about 8 blocks: it plods to the foot of a plant, faces it, and browses, neck stretched up toward it, biting and tugging, for 10 to 20 seconds, then pauses and goes on to the next nearest. Only one is at a plant at a time. It never climbs onto anything a block high: it goes round. |
| 5 | Switch to Survival and walk straight at one, not sneaking. Then sneak up to another, and stand still beside a third. | Not sneaking: within 3 blocks it pulls everything into its shell with a short hiss and a shuffle, and stays in 6 to 12 seconds (it never runs). Then the head comes slowly out (3 seconds, a soft peek sound), it looks about, and goes on with what it was doing (back to its rest, if it was resting). Hit one and it stays in 15 to 25 seconds. Sneaking, standing still, or in Creative: it stays out. |
| 6 | Make Chorus Browse (3 chorus fruit make 4, crafting table or Zoo Workshop). Stand 6 or 7 blocks from one in Survival and hold it (or a chorus fruit). | It plods over to about a block from you, stretches its neck up toward your hand and nibbles at it, for as long as you hold it. Put it away and it stops where it is and goes back to what it was doing. Holding it, you don't startle them. |
| 7 | At the Zoo Workshop make a Warm Pad: 3 end stone bricks in the top row, a magma block in the middle of the row below. | 1 Warm Pad: a whole block, end stone bricks on top with glowing orange seams, softly lit (light 3). Standing on it doesn't hurt you or anything else. Mine it and it drops itself. |
| 8 | Set the Warm Pad into the ground near a wild one and watch its next few basks. | Out of its rest it walks to the pad and basks on it, legs splayed and neck out, and is warm in about half a minute (much quicker than in the open). Then it browses, and once it has cooled (about 2 minutes of browsing) it goes back to the pad. Two never bask on one pad at once: the other basks in the open. |
| 9 | Go to the End's outer islands (through an End gateway). | Wild Starback Tortoises, alone or 2 together, on the end stone among the chorus trees. They bask in the open, browse at the foot of the chorus, and rest under an overhang or a chorus tree. Watch one near an island's edge: it never walks off. |
| 10 | Open the Field Journal on the Starback Tortoise and do its tasks. | Tasks: spot one; watch one bask; watch one browse (at chorus, the feeder or Chorus Browse you hold); come close in Survival and watch it pull into its shell. The care notes cover chorus on the floor, Warm Pads, the low retreat, Chorus Browse in a Basic Feeder, the bouts, and the nest. |
| 11 | Build an exhibit on the flat field: an end stone floor at least 6 x 6, end stone brick walls 2 or more high, open to the sky. Catch 3 wild ones with the Stabilized Carrier (Chorus Browse in your inventory), a male and a female among them, release them inside and measure. Open the Clipboard. | The Clipboard shows "Chorus to browse: 4 of 6 (...)" in red (2 for each, 3 at least), "Warm Pads: 2 of 2 in the floor to bask on", "Retreat: 6 of 6 blocks of floor under a low roof 2 blocks over the floor (...)" and "Mineral Substrate: 3 blocks (a female nests only with 2 or more)". A care warning about the chorus. Add 2 more chorus, measure again: green, and the warning goes. |
| 12 | Stand outside in Creative and watch for 10 minutes. | Each keeps to its bouts: a rest of 1 and a half to 2 and a half minutes under the lean-to, drawn halfway in, then out: to a free Warm Pad to bask till it's warm, then browsing the chorus by turns, now and then to the feeder (it browses at its side), back to a pad once it has cooled, and back under the roof after 3 to 5 minutes. None ends up inside a block, on top of another, up on the feeder or outside the exhibit. |
| 13 | Switch to Survival, step inside and walk right up to one. | Within a block and a half it pulls in with the hiss, as in step 5. Outside the walls, you don't startle them. |
| 14 | Break the lean-to's roof and measure. Then take out both Warm Pads and measure. | A care warning each time: "Starback Tortoises rest under cover: 6 or more blocks of floor under a low roof ..." and "Starback Tortoises bask to warm up before they browse: 2 or more Warm Pads in the floor ...". Comfort can't rise above 50 while either holds. They rest against a wall, and bask in the open. Put them back and measure: the warnings go. |
| 15 | Turn breeding on and skip park days (or wait) till a pair mates. Watch the female. | The mating message says she digs a nest in the Mineral Substrate. She walks to a Mineral Substrate block, digs with her hind feet for about 20 seconds (a soft digging sound), then pats it flat for 6 seconds. The male comes over to her once and stretches his neck toward her, grunting. 3 park days later 2 to 4 hatchlings dig out at the nest (an egg's crack as each first appears): small (0.3 of a female), black with yellow blotches, no stars yet. They go their own way, bask, browse and rest like the grown ones, and grow up in 6 park days. With fewer than 2 Mineral Substrate the breeding page says it needs it. |
